\section{Introduction}
Learned joint source--channel coding (JSCC) maps images to channel symbols and reconstructs them after noisy transmission. Its encoder and decoder jointly determine reconstruction quality under a bandwidth and power budget~\cite{bourtsoulatze2019deep}. SNR-adaptive systems condition this mapping on channel quality, allowing one trained model to operate at several SNRs~\cite{xu2022adaptive,yang2023witt}. Deploying such a system also requires storing its conditioning networks, even when those networks receive a single scalar input.

WITT provides a concrete opportunity to examine this cost. In the author implementation used here, seven encoder gates and seven decoder gates each map scalar SNR to a 384-dimensional vector. These gates are independent of the transmitted image. Their outputs can therefore be evaluated before deployment and stored at selected SNR knots. Runtime interpolation then replaces the learned scalar-to-vector maps. The central question is whether removing their parameters can yield a useful reduction in complete model-file size while preserving reconstruction quality between the stored knots.

We train a local model using the WITT authors' components and compile its scalar gates after training. A 13-node table removes 4,150,272 learned parameters and reduces the serialized model file by 29.72\%. With the checkpoint and deployment choice fixed before testing, its maximum signed PSNR decrease is 0.000186 dB across eight predeclared CIFAR-10/AWGN test conditions. Five of those SNRs coincide with table knots; the remaining three exercise interpolation.

The study contributes a reproducible intervention on WITT's scalar-only gates and an evaluation that separates checkpoint selection, table selection, reload checks, and final testing. In the historical pilot, equal-size five-knot tables have maximum losses of 0.009071 dB with linear interpolation and 0.129533 dB with nearest lookup, motivating the interpolation rule. A shared-SNR control separates repeated scalar computation from parameter removal, and dense validation examines fractional SNRs on a later checkpoint. Together these measurements characterize the storage--reconstruction tradeoff.
