\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Tabulating WITT's scalar SNR gates removes a substantial parameter block while preserving its image-dependent processing. For the selected CIFAR-10/AWGN checkpoint, a 13-node deployment reduces complete serialized storage by 29.72\%, with maximum signed PSNR decrease 0.000186 dB on the frozen eight-point official test. Dense validation and a nearest-neighbor comparison show why evaluating off-knot conditions is essential when compiling a conditional neural network into tables.

\paragraph{Limitations and next steps.}
The evidence covers one architecture, one training trajectory, CIFAR-10 images, simulated AWGN, and CBR $1/3$. Modern libraries, a validation holdout, and a finite update budget differ from the author's training protocol; numerical parity with the published WITT results and convergence remain unestablished. The two checkpoints share one training trajectory. The 13 knots cover 1--13 dB; the implementation clamps outside this range, and domain-exterior behavior is untested. Eight test points and conditional intervals support pointwise conclusions rather than a continuous-SNR guarantee. PSNR measures reconstruction, leaving task-level semantic utility and real-link performance for separate evaluation. Fixed-order pilot timings at 7 dB were mixed: 13 nodes versus original took 40.4 versus 36.2 ms at batch size 1 and 43.7 versus 46.3 ms at batch size 128 (medians of 30 measured forwards after 10 warmups). These observations establish no consistent speed advantage. Further work should examine additional seeds and architectures, rate adaptation, and device-level storage and latency together.
