\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
Tabulating WITT's scalar SNR gates removes a substantial parameter block while preserving its image-dependent processing. For the selected CIFAR-10/AWGN checkpoint, a 13-node deployment reduces complete serialized storage by 29.72\%, with maximum signed PSNR decrease 0.000186 dB on the frozen eight-point official test. Dense validation and a nearest-neighbor comparison show why evaluating off-knot conditions is essential when compiling a conditional neural network into tables.

\paragraph{Limitations and next steps.}
The evidence covers one architecture, one training trajectory, CIFAR-10 images, simulated AWGN, and CBR $1/3$. Modern libraries, a validation holdout, and a finite update budget differ from the author's training protocol; numerical parity with the published WITT results and convergence remain unestablished. The two checkpoints share one training trajectory. The 13 knots cover 1--13 dB; the implementation clamps outside this range, and domain-exterior behavior is untested. Eight test points and conditional intervals support pointwise conclusions rather than a continuous-SNR guarantee. PSNR measures image reconstruction, while downstream semantic-task utility, real radio or satellite links, and other channel models require separate evaluation. Fixed-order pilot timings vary and provide no established latency advantage. Further work should examine additional training seeds and architectures, rate adaptation, and device-level storage and latency together.
